With more and more companies controlling our data, it's more important than ever to keep track
of them. When a password leaks, we change it. But what about data you *can't* change?

Fingerprints, faces, voices and DNA are increasingly used to identify us, from unlocking phones
to ancestry tests. Once one of them leaks, it has leaked for good. You can't rotate your iris
like a password, and a genome exposed in a breach also exposes your relatives.

## What's on the site

**A guide to biometric risk.** The site covers six kinds of identifying data and why each one
matters when it's compromised:

- **Fingerprints**: permanent, and they can be lifted from photos
- **Facial biometrics**: scraped at scale and used for recognition and deepfakes
- **Voice**: enough to clone from a short sample
- **Genomic data**: shared with your family, and impossible to revoke
- **Iris and retina**
- **Behaviour**: how you type, move and use your devices

**A running news tracker.** A curated feed of reporting going back to 2019 on how this data gets
collected, sold, breached and misused. It covers the 23andMe breach, Clearview AI's
facial recognition scraping, voice and video deepfakes, and exposed UK Biobank health records.
